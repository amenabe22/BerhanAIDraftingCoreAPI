## Q2
A. Judges are obliged to vary a contract made between parties having a special relationship when there is change of circumstance.

Under Article 1766 of the Civil Code, the court *may* vary a contract where a special relationship exists and the parties do not agree, compelling them to deal with each other in accordance with equity. This is a discretionary power, not an obligation. Under Article 1763 of the Civil Code, the court may not vary a contract or alter its terms on the ground of equity except in such cases as are expressly provided by law.

## Q3
B. Ethnicity and religion of parties.

Under Article 1734 of the Civil Code, where contract provisions are ambiguous, the common intention of the parties shall be sought, and their general conduct before and after the contract may be considered. Article 1732 of the Civil Code states that contracts shall be interpreted in accordance with good faith, having regard to the loyalty and confidence that should exist between the parties according to business practice. Article 1736 of the Civil Code mandates that contract provisions be interpreted through one another, with each provision given the meaning required by the whole contract, and ambiguous terms given the more likely meaning considering the subject matter. Furthermore, Article 1737 of the Civil Code provides for positive interpretation, meaning provisions capable of two meanings should be given a meaning that renders them effective rather than ineffective. The ethnicity and religion of the parties are not mentioned as factors to consider in contract interpretation.

## Q5
D. The reasonable person standard is employed to assess the amount of damages which is payable to the creditor. Under Article 1799 of the Civil Code, damages shall be equal to the damage which non-performance would normally have caused to the creditor in the eyes of a reasonable person.

## Q6
D. The creditor is debarred from claiming the secondary guarantor before asserting against the principal debtor and the main guarantor.

Under Article 1950 of the Civil Code, a secondary guarantor is in the same position towards the guarantor as a simple guarantor is towards the principal debtor. This implies that the secondary guarantor can raise the benefit of discussion, meaning the creditor must first proceed against the main guarantor before claiming from the secondary guarantor. The statement that the creditor is debarred from claiming the secondary guarantor before asserting against the principal debtor and the main guarantor is therefore true. The question asks for the statement that is *not* true.

## Q7
C. The creditor always has the burden of proving that the apparent act is a simulation to challenge debtor’s transaction.

Under Article 1994 of the Civil Code, a creditor may have established, by judicial decision, that a transaction effected by a debtor was a simulated one which, by agreement, was not intended to be carried out. This implies that the creditor needs to prove the simulation. However, the statement says the creditor *always* has the burden, which may not be true in all circumstances, especially if there are legal presumptions or other mechanisms that shift the burden of proof.

Under Article 1987 of the Civil Code, the performance by the debtor of his obligations shall be secured by all his assets, with the exception of those which cannot be attached at law. This means that not all assets of the debtor may be subject to attachment.

Under Article 1993 of the Civil Code, a creditor may, with the authorisation of the court, exercise as representative of the debtor all the rights of the debtor so as to prevent such impoverishment of the debtor as would jeopardize the payment of the debt. This provision, along with others like Article 1995 (Debtor's fraud) and Article 1996 (Fraudulent acts), indicates that legal provisions generally favor the creditor in protecting their rights against a debtor's actions that could jeopardize the debt.

## Q8
B. Partial performance of the indivisible obligation by one of the debtor validly discharges his part of obligation.

Under Article 1917 of the Civil Code, the provisions regarding joint obligations apply by analogy to obligations that are indivisible due to their nature. However, Article 1918 of the Civil Code states that obligations which are neither joint nor indivisible are divided between the persons liable, and each debtor is liable for their lawful share. This implies that for an indivisible obligation, partial performance by one debtor would not discharge their part, as the obligation itself cannot be divided.

## Q15
D. Mr. A can transfer full rights of his share to Mr. X without the assent of his fellow partners.

Under Article 219(1) of the Commercial Code, shares may not be transferred to third parties except with the consent of all general partners and a majority of the limited partners. While Article 197(1) of the Commercial Code allows a partner to transfer beneficial interests and rights in his share to a third party without the consent of other partners, Article 197(2) clarifies that such a transfer does not bind the partnership and the third party does not acquire full rights of a partner. Therefore, transferring full rights of a share requires the consent of the other partners.

## Q16
C. A right of pre-emption may not be created for more than ten years, since such agreement affects the public interest for the free transfer of goods. Under Article 1419 of the Civil Code, a right of pre-emption shall be exercised within two months from the beneficiary being informed of the owner's intention to sell. The parties may extend this period to one year by agreement, but if a period exceeding one year has been agreed, it shall be reduced to one year.

## Q17

This definition does not limit possession to only physical control, implying that other forms of control may also constitute possession.

## Q18

This principle aligns with the concept of occupation or first possession for acquiring ownership of unowned things.

## Q19

Under Article 1265 of the Civil Code, decisions regarding a jointly owned thing are taken by a majority vote of owners representing a majority share. This indicates that a single joint owner cannot unilaterally block collective decisions.

Under Article 826 of the Civil Code, the succession of a deceased person includes the rights and obligations that form the inheritance, unless such rights and obligations terminate with the death of the deceased. Article 1005 of the Civil Code further clarifies that the liquidator establishes what the succession is made up of by drawing up an inventory. The testator and the witnesses must immediately sign the will or affix their thumb mark thereon.

## Q26
B. Vicarious liability.

Product liability, strict liability, and the absence of fault not being a defense are all related concepts under Ethiopian law concerning liability for manufactured goods. Under Article 2085 of the Civil Code, a person who manufactures and sells goods for profit is liable for damage caused by their normal use. Article 2086 further clarifies that those legally liable for damage caused by manufactured goods cannot escape liability by proving they committed no offense, or that the cause of damage was unknown, or that it was not in their power to prevent the damage, or that the damage was due to a third party. This indicates a form of strict liability where the absence of fault is not a defense. Liability is imposed on producers in these cases.

Vicarious liability, on the other hand, refers to a situation where one person is held responsible for the actions or omissions of another person. While the Commercial Code, Article 228, mentions that a company can be held jointly and severally liable with a partner or employee for damages caused during the performance of the company's work, this is a distinct concept from the liability of a producer for defects in manufactured goods.

## Q27
A. In tort law all types of harm attract legal liability.

Under Article 2028 of the Civil Code, "Whosoever causes damage to another by an offence shall make it good." This general principle indicates that causing damage through an offense leads to liability. However, the statement "all types of harm attract legal liability" is too broad and not explicitly supported as universally true for every conceivable harm without qualification.

B. Existence of damage is a precondition in order to claim remedy in tort law.

Under Article 2028 of the Civil Code, liability arises when a person "causes damage to another by an offence." This implies that damage is a prerequisite for liability in extra-contractual matters. Additionally, Article 1892 of the Civil Code, while discussing penalties, distinguishes between actual damage and the penalty due, further suggesting that damage is a core concept in claiming remedies.

C. Age is a defense that exempt from liability.

Article 303 of the Family Code states that "Where the minor had caused damage to another person by his unlawful acts or enriched himself unlawfully, he shall be liable in accordance with the provisions of the Civil Code relating to Extra-Contractual Liability and Unlawful Enrichment." This indicates that a minor can be held liable for damage caused by unlawful acts, suggesting that age alone does not automatically exempt from extra-contractual liability.

Therefore, the statement that is not true is A.

## Q28
A. Fault of the victim totally exempts the tort-feasor or at least reduces the extent of liability that the latter owes to the victim.

Under Article 2086 of the Civil Code, persons legally liable for creating an abnormal risk or for damage caused by animals, buildings, machines, motor vehicles, or manufactured goods may be relieved of their liability entirely or in part if the damage is due solely or partly to the fault of the victim. Furthermore, under Article 2098 of the Civil Code, where damage is partly due to the fault of the victim, the victim is entitled to partial compensation only. The extent to which the damage shall be made good is determined by considering all circumstances, particularly the contribution and gravity of the faults committed.

## Q30
D. The principle of equality implies equality of treatment although they are not in equal circumstances.

Under Article 4 of the Amharic Revised Criminal Code, criminal law applies equally to all without distinction based on social status, race, ethnicity, color, gender, language, religion, political or other opinion, wealth, origin, or other status. Differences among offenders are made only when there is a fundamental right stipulated in this law, originating from international law or the constitution, or when this law stipulates that the severity of the crime, the degree of fault, or the age, circumstances, or special personal characteristics of the offender, or the danger the crime has caused to society, should be taken into account. This indicates that while the law applies equally, treatment can differ based on specific circumstances. Therefore, the statement that equality implies equality of treatment *although they are not in equal circumstances* is incorrect, as differences in circumstances can lead to differentiated treatment under the law.

Under Article 2 of the English Revised Criminal Code, the principle of legality states that the Court may not treat as a crime and punish any act or omission which is not prohibited by law, nor impose penalties or measures other than those prescribed by law. This supports the statement that according to the principle of legality, there is no crime and punishment unless specified by law.

## Q31
A. Crimes committed by members of the Ethiopian defense forces against the ‘ordinary law’ of a foreign country.

Under Article 15 of the Revised Criminal Code, where a member of the Ethiopian Defence Forces commits a crime against the ordinary law in a foreign country, they are subject to the ordinary law and territorial jurisdiction of that country if arrested and tried there. If they take refuge in Ethiopia, they are tried in accordance with the provisions of the Code. This indicates that the primary jurisdiction for such crimes is the foreign country where the crime was committed, not Ethiopia's principal jurisdiction.

## Q32
C. The existence of bad thought to violate criminal law by itself is sufficient to punish a person although not manifested.

Under Article 23 of the Revised Criminal Code, a crime is only completed when all its legal, material, and moral ingredients are present. This indicates that a "bad thought" alone, without a manifested act, is not sufficient for a crime to be complete. Furthermore, Article 23 states that a crime is an act which is prohibited and made punishable by law, and an act consists of the commission of what is prohibited or the omission of what is prescribed by law. This emphasizes the requirement of an external manifestation or conduct.

## Q33
D. 'Amnesty' necessarily applies after conviction.

Under Article 230 of the Revised Criminal Code, amnesty prevents the initiation or continuation of any criminal prosecution from the time it is granted. If a penalty has been imposed, amnesty cancels the penalty and any other criminal consequences, and the conviction is erased from the criminal record. This indicates that amnesty can be granted before or after conviction. In contrast, Article 229 of the Revised Criminal Code states that pardon applies to a penalty imposed by a court, implying it is a post-conviction procedure.

## Q35
C. Persons under the age of nine

Under Article 48 of the Revised Criminal Code, criminal responsibility is attributed to the offender who is accountable for their actions. While the article does not explicitly state that persons under nine are absolutely free from criminal law, it implies that age is a factor in criminal responsibility.

This reinforces the idea that individuals under nine are not held criminally responsible in the same way.
