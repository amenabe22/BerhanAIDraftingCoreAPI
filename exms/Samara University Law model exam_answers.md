## Q1
C. A contract can be formed even between absent parties as long as there are appropriate offer and acceptance between the contracting parties.
Under Article 1679 of the Civil Code, a contract is formed when the parties express their agreement. Article 1680 further clarifies that consent may be expressed by words, by conduct, or by silence where such silence is deemed to constitute consent. The law does not require physical presence for the formation of a contract.

## Q2
D. Determining a contract's requirements regarding legality of a contract.
Under Article 1716 of the Civil Code, the object of a contract must be lawful. Parties cannot contractually agree to an unlawful object.

## Q3
B. When the defendant's negligence is shown, remedies may be available, regardless of whether there is actual or perceived material damage to the plaintiff's interest.
Under Article 2028 of the Civil Code, a person who causes damage to another by an unlawful act or omission is liable to make good the damage. The concept of "damage" in extra-contractual liability generally refers to actual harm. However, the specific wording of the question implies that remedies might be available even without material damage, which aligns with certain aspects of moral damage or injunctions.

## Q4
B. Uncertainty as to the exact amount of damage caused is a defense for a perpetrator to escape liability under the Ethiopian tort law.
Under Article 2090 of the Civil Code, where the exact amount of damage cannot be assessed, the court shall fix it at its discretion. This indicates that uncertainty of the exact amount does not absolve liability.

## Q5
D. All.
Under Article 2027 of the Civil Code, the purpose of compensation is to place the injured party in the position he would have been in had the tort not been committed. This can include monetary compensation, but also other forms of restitution. The existence or absence of intention is relevant in determining fault and the extent of liability (Article 2028, 2030, 2031 of the Civil Code). Strict liability, as per Article 2060 of the Civil Code, does not require fault.

## Q6
D. All.
Cancellation (Article 1790 of the Civil Code), termination (Article 1806 of the Civil Code), and novation (Article 1960 of the Civil Code) all generally require the consent of both parties to extinguish a contract.

## Q7
C. Enforcement of parties' commitment.
Injunction (Article 2091 of the Civil Code), compensation (Article 2027 of the Civil Code), and restitution (Article 2027 of the Civil Code) are all concepts related to Ethiopian tort law. Enforcement of parties' commitment is primarily a concept of contract law.

## Q8
C. A contract can be formed even between absent parties as long as there are appropriate offer and acceptance between the contracting parties.
Under Article 1679 of the Civil Code, a contract is formed when the parties express their agreement. Article 1680 further clarifies that consent may be expressed by words, by conduct, or by silence where such silence is deemed to constitute consent. The law does not require physical presence for the formation of a contract.

## Q9
D. The interest harmed must have always monetary value.
Under Article 2027 of the Civil Code, compensation is due for damage, which can include both material and moral damage. Moral damage does not necessarily have a monetary value.

## Q10
B. The Commercial Code is a self – contained law on traders and there is no need for the application of the Civil Code on traders.
Under Article 2 of the Commercial Code, the provisions of the Civil Code shall apply to commercial matters where the Commercial Code contains no provision. This indicates that the Commercial Code is not entirely self-contained and the Civil Code can apply.

## Q11
A. It is not required to be registered.
Under Article 271 of the Commercial Code, a joint venture is not subject to registration.

## Q12
A. Setting the period of limitation.
Under Article 1845 of the Civil Code, periods of limitation are fixed by law and cannot be modified by agreement of the parties.

## Q13
D. Vicarious liability.
Strict liability (Article 2060 of the Civil Code), liability without fault, and liability which does not depend on actual negligence or intent to harm are all related concepts where fault of the actor is not a prerequisite for liability. Vicarious liability (Article 2087 of the Civil Code) is a different concept where one person is held liable for the acts of another.

## Q14
A. Hanna cannot claim the watch from Yafet.
Under Article 1132 of the Civil Code, a person who acquires a movable in good faith from a person who is not the owner, by way of onerous title, acquires ownership of the movable. Yafet acquired the watch in good faith in an open market.

## Q15
B. The general manager of the company.
Under Article 141 of the Commercial Code, the memorandum of association of a share company must state the name, head office, business purpose, capital, and duration of the company, but not the general manager.

## Q16
D. All.
Under Article 124 of the Commercial Code, a business includes corporeal elements (goods, equipment) and incorporeal elements (patents, copyrights, goodwill).

## Q17
D. All.
Under Article 300 of the Commercial Code, liquidators, the board of directors, and persons nominated by the court can call a general meeting of shareholders.

## Q18
D. All.
Under Article 140 of the Commercial Code, founders are persons who sign the memorandum and articles of association and subscribe the whole capital. For public share companies, those who sign the prospectus are also founders. The term can also encompass those who initiate the plans for the company's formation.

## Q19
A. Special meeting is a meeting in which all shareholders shall attend.
Under Article 301 of the Commercial Code, special meetings are for specific purposes and do not necessarily require all shareholders to attend. The rules for quorum and majority for extraordinary meetings apply to special meetings (Article 302 of the Commercial Code).

## Q20
D. All.
Under Article 211 of the Commercial Code, partnerships are legal persons. They can be commercial or non-commercial (Article 212 of the Commercial Code).

## Q21
C. All.
Under Article 221 of the Commercial Code, partners in a general partnership are jointly, severally, and personally liable for the firm's debts. This implies unlimited liability.

## Q22
D. All.
Under Article 271 of the Commercial Code, a joint venture is not required to be in written form. Under Article 272 of the Commercial Code, it does not have legal personality. The property of the partners remains their own, but it is used for the joint venture.

## Q23
D. All.
Under Article 440 of the Commercial Code, shares in a private limited company are freely transferable among shareholders. Transfer to outsiders requires a majority vote of at least 75% (Article 441 of the Commercial Code). Shares are also transmitted to heirs (Article 442 of the Commercial Code).

## Q24
B. Commercial in nature.
Under Article 1 of the Commercial Code, the code applies to commercial matters and commercial persons. Companies formed under the Commercial Code are inherently commercial.

## Q25
D. All.
A share is a financial asset, gives rights to the holder, and a shareholder is a part-owner of the company.

## Q26
B. It is the contracting parties who enjoy rights and assume obligations from the contract they have concluded.
Under Article 1952 of the Civil Code, a contract creates rights and obligations only between the contracting parties.

## Q27
A. The fulfillment of a conditional contract.
Good faith is generally considered in the interpretation of contracts (Article 1733 of the Civil Code), cancellation of contracts (Article 1790 of the Civil Code), and novation of a contract (Article 1960 of the Civil Code). The fulfillment of a conditional contract is governed by the conditions themselves (Article 1739 of the Civil Code).

## Q28
A. A person intentionally fails to perform his contractual obligation.
This situation primarily gives rise to contractual liability (Article 1791 of the Civil Code), not extra-contractual liability. The other options describe situations that could lead to extra-contractual liability.

## Q29
B. Defect in the formation of a contract.
Invalidation (nullity) of a contract (Article 1808 of the Civil Code) is associated with defects in its formation, such as lack of consent, capacity, or unlawful object. Compensation is associated with non-performance.

## Q30
D. Immorality of object of contract.
Mistake (Article 1697 of the Civil Code), duress (Article 1704 of the Civil Code), and fraud (Article 1700 of the Civil Code) are vices of consent. Immorality of the object of a contract (Article 1716 of the Civil Code) relates to the object of the contract, not the consent itself.

## Q31
D. All type of marriage follows the same celebration formality.
Under Article 10 of the Revised Family Code, there are different forms of marriage (civil, religious, customary), and they may have different celebration formalities. However, they generally share the same validity requirements (Article 11 of the Revised Family Code), are registered (Article 12 of the Revised Family Code), and produce similar legal consequences (Article 41 of the Revised Family Code).

## Q32
B. time of celebration of marriage.
Under Article 41 of the Revised Family Code, marriage produces its effects from the day of its celebration.

## Q33
C. all property acquired by during marriage by onerous title.
Under Article 57 of the Revised Family Code, in the absence of a marriage contract or if it is invalid, all property acquired by either spouse during marriage by onerous title is common property.

## Q34
E. Each of the spouse shall freely administer his/her personal property.
Under Article 60 of the Revised Family Code, each spouse retains the right to administer, enjoy, and dispose of their personal property.

## Q35
D. Borrowing birr 400 from another person.
Under Article 63 of the Revised Family Code, a spouse may freely undertake obligations that do not exceed the normal needs of the household. Borrowing a small sum for personal use would generally fall under this. Renting out a commonly owned house (Article 62 of the Revised Family Code) or exchanging a valuable commonly owned item (Article 62 of the Revised Family Code) would require the consent of both spouses. Giving a small gift is generally permissible.

## Q36
C. If the child is born 300 days after the court declared the absence of the husband.
Under Article 135 of the Revised Family Code, the husband is presumed to be the father of a child born within 180 days of the marriage or within 300 days of its dissolution. If the husband is declared absent, and the child is born 300 days after this declaration, the presumption of paternity would likely not apply.

## Q37
C. An obligation to supply maintenance does not exist between brothers and sisters.
Under Article 183 of the Revised Family Code, the obligation to supply maintenance exists between ascendants and descendants, and between spouses. It can also exist between persons related by affinity in the direct line (Article 184 of the Revised Family Code). The law does not generally impose a maintenance obligation between siblings.

## Q38
A. the father of the child.
Under Article 209 of the Revised Family Code, the father is the legal administrator of the child's property and the guardian of the child.

## Q39
B. Patrimony is attached to its holder.
Under Article 1456 of the Civil Code, every person has a patrimony, and it is attached to the person.

## Q40
C. It is a bundle of right.
Ownership is considered a bundle of rights, including the right to use, enjoy, and dispose of the property (Article 1204 of the Civil Code).

## Q41
D. The possessor or the holder should use a reasonable force.
Under Article 1149 of the Civil Code, a possessor may use reasonable force to defend his possession.

## Q42
D. The buyer should purchase the thing from a person who has no a valid title.
Under Article 1132 of the Civil Code, to acquire ownership through possession in good faith, the person must acquire the movable from a person who is not the owner, by way of onerous title, and in good faith.

## Q43
C. Snatching a good and lapse of a period of limitation for a legal action.
Snatching a good is an unlawful act and does not legitimately transfer ownership. Lapse of a period of limitation for a legal action might prevent recovery but does not constitute a legitimate cause of transfer and acquisition of ownership. The other options are legitimate means of transferring and acquiring ownership.

## Q44
D. It is aright in rem.
Under Article 1300 of the Civil Code, servitude is a right in rem, meaning it is a right attached to a thing (land) rather than a person.

## Q45
C. Properties are expropriated in the interest of the public at large.
Under Article 1462 of the Civil Code, expropriation is for public interest. It does not require the consent of the owner, but compensation is due (Article 1463 of the Civil Code).

## Q46
C. Victim contribution to the materialization of damage.
Under Article 2033 of the Civil Code, if the victim's fault contributed to the damage, the compensation may be reduced or refused. This is a departure from the rule of equivalence.

## Q47
D. Unlawful termination of a worker contract of employment.
Unlawful termination of an employment contract gives rise to contractual liability under labor law, not extra-contractual liability (tort). The other options are sources of extra-contractual liability (fault - Article 2028 of the Civil Code; activities/things possessed - Article 2060 of the Civil Code; responsibility for third parties - Article 2087 of the Civil Code).

## Q48
C. Abuse of power.
Control test (Article 2087 of the Civil Code) and deep pockets are rationales for vicarious liability. Analogy for causation is a general legal reasoning principle. Abuse of power is a type of wrongful conduct, not a rationale for holding one person liable for another's wrongful conduct.

## Q49
C. Deceased person’s brother.
Under Article 2035 of the Civil Code, maintenance can be claimed by the spouse, descendants, and ascendants of the deceased. Siblings are not typically included.

## Q50
D. Forced performance.
Monetary compensation (Article 2027 of the Civil Code), restitution (Article 2027 of the Civil Code), and remedial publication are remedies under extra-contractual liability. Forced performance is a remedy for breach of contract (Article 1776 of the Civil Code).

## Q51
B. Criminal liability and forced labor.
Under Article 1791 of the Civil Code, non-performance of a contract can lead to cancellation, compensation, or forced performance. It does not generally entail criminal liability or forced labor.

## Q52
D. All.
Under Article 1936 of the Civil Code, a guarantor who has paid has a right to claim indemnity from the principal debtor. Under Article 1937 of the Civil Code, the guarantor may set up defenses that the principal debtor could have raised. Under Article 1935 of the Civil Code, the guarantor has the right to summon the creditor to proceed against the principal debtor.

## Q53
D. Counter guarantor.
Secondary guarantor, simple guarantor, and joint guarantor are all types of guarantors in relation to the principal debt. A counter guarantor is a guarantor for another guarantor.

## Q54
B. Employers for employees.
Under Article 2088 of the Civil Code, employers are liable for the damage caused by their employees in the course of their employment.

## Q55
B. Ethiopian courts are not prohibited from interpreting contracts where interpretation is genuinely called for because of ambiguity, vagueness or contradiction of contractual provisions.
Under Article 1714(2) of the Civil Code, courts may not make a contract under the guise of interpretation. However, Article 1732 of the Civil Code allows for interpretation where the terms are ambiguous or contradictory.

## Q56
A. Object, consent capacity, and form when the law requires.
Under Article 1678 of the Civil Code, the essential conditions for the validity of a contract are consent, capacity, object, and form where required by law.

## Q57
D. The immovable nature of the property.
The rule of *paterna patenis materna maternis* (paternal property to paternal heirs, maternal property to maternal heirs) is a rule of succession that applies to immovable property. The will of the testator (Article 857 of the Civil Code), intestacy (Article 826 of the Civil Code), and absence of descendants (Article 842 of the Civil Code) are relevant to succession. The immovable nature of the property is a condition for the application of the rule, not an irrelevant condition.

## Q58
D. All.
Under Article 904 of the Civil Code, the court may invalidate a will made under undue influence, taking into account the condition of the testator, the identity of the person exerting influence, and the gravity of the influence.

## Q59
C. The contract of marriage is in harmony with mandatory provisions of the law.
The law regulates the personal and pecuniary effects of marriage when the marriage is valid and in harmony with mandatory provisions. If the marriage contract is invalid (Article 11 of the Revised Family Code) or there is no contract, the law's provisions on marriage effects would not apply in the same way.

## Q60
D. The will contains additions in the margins or between the lines.
Under Article 894 of the Civil Code for public wills and Article 898 of the Civil Code for holographic wills, additions in the margins or between the lines can lead to nullity. The other options are also grounds for nullity for specific types of wills.

## Q61
C. Error.
Under Article 901 of the Civil Code, fraud, duress, and undue influence can vitiate consent with respect to a will. Error is not explicitly listed as a vice of consent for wills in the same way as for contracts.

## Q62
B. Adamu can benefit from the legacy even without complying with the requirement.
Under Article 910 of the Civil Code, conditions that are impossible, unlawful, or immoral are deemed not to have been written. Changing religion as a requirement for a legacy would likely be considered immoral or unlawful.

## Q63
C. Sara.
Under Article 929 of the Civil Code, if a testator makes two wills that are incompatible, the later will revokes the earlier one to the extent of the incompatibility.

## Q64
D. None.
Dissolution of marriage (Article 927 of the Civil Code), death of the legatee before the testator (Article 922 of the Civil Code), and birth of a child after the will is made (Article 926 of the Civil Code) can all result in the lapse of a will.

## Q65
D. A and C.
Under Article 1038 of the Civil Code, the merger of the property of the inheritance with the personal property of the heirs can negatively affect the interests of both the creditors of the deceased and the personal creditors of the heirs.

## Q66
D. All.
Under Article 1040 of the Civil Code, an heir may accept or renounce succession through an agent. Under Article 1044 of the Civil Code, creditors of an heir who renounced a succession to their disadvantage can apply to the court for invalidation. Under Article 1041 of the Civil Code, acceptance or renunciation cannot be conditional.

## Q67
C. Creditors of the deceased can claim a payment from a properties part of succession after the closure of liquidation.
Under Article 1070 of the Civil Code, after liquidation, creditors of the deceased can claim payment from the heirs up to the value of the property received.

## Q68
A. It shall open after the closure of liquidation.
Under Article 826 of the Civil Code, succession opens at the death of the deceased. Liquidation occurs after the opening of succession.

## Q69
B. parents for their minor children.
Under Article 2089 of the Civil Code, parents are liable for the damage caused by their minor children.

## Q70
C. Movable by nature.
Rules governing transactions involving corporeal chattels (movables) apply to things that are movable by nature (Article 1126 of the Civil Code). Immovables by destination (Article 1137 of the Civil Code) and accessories of immovables (Article 1137 of the Civil Code) are treated as immovables.

## Q71
D. Mortgage.
Mortgage (Article 3041 of the Civil Code) is a security right over property and does not transfer ownership. Acquisitive prescription (Article 1168 of the Civil Code) and usucapion (Article 1168 of the Civil Code) are ways to acquire ownership. Loss of physical control can lead to loss of possession, which can eventually lead to loss of ownership.

## Q72
D. declaration.
Restitution (Article 2027 of the Civil Code), injunction (Article 2091 of the Civil Code), and enforcement of parties' commitment (though primarily contractual, can have tortious implications in some contexts) are related to Ethiopian tort law. "Declaration" is a broader legal concept.

## Q73
D. Cellphone.
Aircraft (Article 1140 of the Civil Code), ships (Article 1140 of the Civil Code), and businesses (Article 124 of the Commercial Code) are considered special movables. A cellphone is an ordinary movable.

## Q74
C. The obligation to take measure of publicity with a view to informing the owner of the fact that he found the chattel.
Under Article 1177 of the Civil Code, the finder of a lost movable has an obligation to take measures of publicity to inform the owner.

## Q75
D. the interest harmed must always have monetary value.
Under Article 2027 of the Civil Code, compensation is due for damage, which can include both material and moral damage. Moral damage does not necessarily have a monetary value.
