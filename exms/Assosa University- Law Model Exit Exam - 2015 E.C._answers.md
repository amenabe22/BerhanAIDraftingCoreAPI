## Q1
A. Object, form, consent and capacity
Under Article 1678 of the Civil Code, the essential conditions for the formation of a contract are the consent of the parties, their capacity to contract, a definite object which forms the subject-matter of the contract, and that the contract be made in the form prescribed by law, if any. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1678]

## Q2
In principle, courts are not allowed to vary a contract between the parties.
Under Article 1731 of the Civil Code, courts may not vary contracts. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1731]

## Q3
Ethnicity and religion of parties.
Under Article 1733 of the Civil Code, courts shall interpret contracts according to the common intention of the parties rather than the literal meaning of the terms. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1733] Article 1734 of the Civil Code states that the provisions of a contract shall be interpreted in the light of the contract as a whole and in accordance with the purpose of the contract. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1734] Article 1735 of the Civil Code provides that courts shall take into account the usages and practices followed in the place where the contract was made and in the branch of activity to which the contract relates. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1735] Article 1736 of the Civil Code states that courts shall interpret contracts in good faith. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1736]

## Q4
Performance of obligation of diligence by authorized third party may validly discharge the debtor from his obligations.
Under Article 1740 of the Civil Code, an obligation may be performed by a third party even against the will of the creditor, unless the creditor has a material interest in the performance being rendered by the debtor himself. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1740]

## Q5
The remedy of damages does not necessarily require fault to establish the debtors’ liability.
Under Article 1790 of the Civil Code, a debtor who fails to perform his obligation shall be liable for the damage he causes to the creditor unless he proves that such failure is due to a cause not imputable to him. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1790]

## Q6
Counter guarantor disfavors the creditor than secondary one.
Under Article 1941 of the Civil Code, a counter-guarantee is a contract whereby a person guarantees the obligations of a guarantor. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1941] Under Article 1939 of the Civil Code, a secondary guarantor is a person who guarantees the obligations of a guarantor. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1939]

## Q7
The creditor always has the burden of proving that the apparent act is a simulation to challenge debtor’s transaction.
Under Article 1996 of the Civil Code, a creditor may challenge acts made by his debtor in fraud of his rights. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1996]

## Q8
Partial performance of the indivisible obligation by one of the debtor validly discharges his part of obligation.
Under Article 1896 of the Civil Code, an obligation is indivisible where its object cannot be divided without altering its nature or value. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1896] Under Article 1897 of the Civil Code, an indivisible obligation may arise from the nature of the object, from the agreement of the parties, or from the law. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1897] Under Article 1898 of the Civil Code, each of the debtors of an indivisible obligation is bound to perform the whole obligation. [Source: Civil Code | Article 1898]
