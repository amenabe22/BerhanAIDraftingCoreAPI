## Q9
A) properties acquired by onerous title after marriage through the exchange of personal property
Property acquired by onerous title after marriage through the exchange of personal property is generally considered personal property only if the court, at the request of one of the spouses, decides it to be so. Under Article 58 of the English Family Code, property acquired by onerous title by one spouse after marriage, through exchange for personally owned property or with personally owned money, is personal property only if a court decision declares it as such. Otherwise, property acquired by onerous title after marriage is generally common property.

## Q29
C) The contract will have binding effect as between the spouses but not third parties
A copy of the contract of marriage must be deposited in the court or with the office of civil status. While the contract of marriage itself must be in writing and attested by four witnesses to be effective, the Family Code does not explicitly state that failure to deposit the contract invalidates it. However, Article 46 of the English Family Code states that spouses cannot impose obligations on third parties through their contract of marriage. This implies that while the contract may be binding between the spouses, its effects on third parties might be limited if not properly deposited.
